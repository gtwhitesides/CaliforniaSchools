Standing out

    Filtering

    Only school-level rows are kept — Type ID 07, 09, or 10. District, county, and state summary rows are dropped so nothing is double-counted. Student groups 001 (All Students), 031 (socioeconomically disadvantaged) and 111 (not socioeconomically disadvantaged) are retained; the other fifty-odd groups are discarded as the file streams past. Smarter Balanced English is Test ID 1, math 2, CAST 17. Everything joins on the 14-digit county–district–school code, and each file is filed under whatever year its own Test Year column reports.

    A note on naming. What this tool calls English, the state calls ELA — English language arts/literacy. The label is plainer here, but if you cross-reference against CDE record layouts, DataQuest, or a district's own reporting, look for ELA. The underlying measure is identical.

    Year-over-year change

    Change measures subtract the comparison year from the selected year for the same school and grade. Two things distort them. Schools reconstitute, merge, and change CDS codes, so a code that exists in both years is not guaranteed to be the same school community. And a school's grade-11 cohort in 2025 is a different set of students from its grade-11 cohort in 2024 — this is a change in the school's result, not growth by individual students. True cohort growth would require following grade 7 in 2024 into grade 8 in 2025, which these files support but the tool does not currently do.

    Family income measures

    What this tool calls lower-income students, the state calls socioeconomically disadvantaged, or SED. The plainer wording is a simplification: the state’s definition also covers students whose parents did not complete high school, and students who are foster or homeless — so it is somewhat broader than income alone. The share is computed as students tested in group 031 divided by students tested in group 001. That is a proxy for school poverty derived from who sat the test, not the official unduplicated pupil percentage a school reports for funding. The two track each other closely but are not the same number, and the proxy should not be quoted as a school's poverty rate.

    The equity gap subtracts the disadvantaged group's result from the non-disadvantaged group's. Both groups must clear the 11-student reporting floor, so schools that are nearly all low-income — or nearly all affluent — drop out of gap measures entirely. The surviving sample is biased toward economically mixed schools, which is precisely the population where gaps are most visible. Read the gap chart as a statement about mixed-income schools, not about all schools.

    Scale scores versus percentages

    Percent met or exceeded is scored against a grade-specific cut, so it means the same thing in third grade and eleventh. Mean scale score does not: Smarter Balanced uses a vertical scale that climbs with grade, and CAST scale scores are a different scale entirely with no relationship to the Smarter Balanced numbers. The tool refuses to fit those two against each other.

    Science coverage

    CAST is given in grades 5, 8, and one high school grade of the school's choosing. Smarter Balanced runs 3–8 and 11. Science joins cleanly to English and math at grades 5 and 8, where the same students sat both tests the same spring. Anywhere else the cohorts differ, and the tool says so.

    Reading a zoomed chart

    Axis range and drag-zoom change only what you can see, never what is calculated. The fitted line stays fitted to every school passing your filters, and each school’s position relative to it is unchanged — so a dot’s colour means the same thing zoomed in as zoomed out. Be careful reading magnitudes off a zoomed chart, though: cropping a percentage axis from 0–100 down to 30–50 makes a two-point difference look like a chasm. The full range is the honest default; the zoom is for finding a school in a crowd, not for judging how far apart two schools are.

    Color and suppression

    Points are shaded by residual from the fitted line. Given a school's X, is its Y above or below what the statewide pattern predicts? Nothing is reported for any group under 11 students, so the smallest schools are absent by construction rather than missing by error.
